# Code is Commodity — Ideas Will Win

Code is getting so cheap that ideas will win.

Not *how* you build it. **What** you're building.

That's the shift.

---

## The Commodity Shift

A few years ago, writing code was the skill that separated the signal from the noise. You needed to be a developer, or you were a consumer. That wall between builder and user was real, and it meant something.

But that wall is dissolving.

LLMs, Copilot agents, auto-generated scaffolds, no-code platforms — the cost of turning an idea into a working prototype has collapsed. What used to take a senior engineer a week can now be assembled in an afternoon, often by someone who's never written a line of code.

The barrier to creation is getting closer to the floor.

---

## When Software Becomes Commodity, Fundamentals Win

Here's the paradox: as software becomes easier to build, the people who don't know code but can clearly define outcomes will be the most valuable.

Why? Because the bottleneck has moved.

It's no longer *can you build it?*
It's *what should you build?*

The fundamentals that matter now:

### Business Fundamentals
- What problem are we actually solving?
- Who feels this pain enough to pay for a solution?
- What's the actual unit economics of the product?

### Financial Literacy
- How does value flow from customer to company to margin?
- What's our burn relative to traction?
- When does "growth" become "value" and when is it just "burn"?

### Value Flow Awareness
- What's the actual job-to-be-done?
- Where's the leverage point that creates disproportionate impact?
- What are we optimizing for: engagement, retention, revenue, or actual value?

---

## I'm Moving My Focus

The focus is shifting from code to **value**.

The people with the right ideas, grounded in fundamentals, will build what matters next.

Because the best code in the world doesn't matter if you're solving the wrong problem.

---

## What This Means for Builders

If you're a developer reading this: you could actually have an edge if you understand these concepts as well as code.

If you're a founder or operator: double down on your fundamentals. The builders will come. And you could eventually be one.

If you're a customer: pay attention to who's actually thinking about your problem — not who's just shipping.

---

## The New Moat

In a world where code is cheap, what's expensive?

- **Clarity** — knowing what's worth building
- **Judgment** — knowing what's worth ignoring
- **Fundamentals** — understanding business, finance, and value flow
- **Experience** — knowing when to bet, when to cut, when to pivot

These don't get automated away. They get sharpened.

---

## Bottom Line

The era of "I'm a developer, I'll figure out the rest" is over.

The era of "I know what problem to solve, and I have a solution I'm building for it" is just beginning.

Build what matters. Not what's easy.
